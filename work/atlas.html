Case study — Northbeam

Atlas
observability console

Northbeam had forty-one dashboards and no answer to the only question that matters at 03:00: is it just me? Atlas is one screen that answers it — traffic, errors, latency and saturation, with a trace one click away.

01 — The brief Forty-one dashboards, zero answers, and an on-call rotation that had stopped trusting any of them.

Every team at Northbeam had built the dashboard it needed. Collectively that came to forty-one of them, and by the third week of an on-call shift an engineer knew which six were worth opening. The other thirty-five were decoration.

The cost showed up in the incident timeline. Of a median 14-minute time to detect, most of it was spent establishing whether the problem was real at all — flipping between six tools, trying to agree with yourself before you paged anyone.

The data was already there, at full fidelity. What was missing was a screen built around the question rather than around the metrics each team happened to own.

02 — What I built Golden signals on one screen, and a trace one click from wherever you were standing.

Atlas opens to a single table: one row per service, four columns — traffic, errors, latency, saturation. Nothing else competes for attention until you ask for it.

  • Golden-signals overview — every service on one screen, with the delta that matters (vs. one hour ago) instead of a sparkline you have to interpret.
  • Trace-first drill-down — service → endpoint → span waterfall → the exact request. The path from "something is wrong" to "this is the query" is three clicks and no context switch.
  • Streamed aggregation — the browser never receives raw spans. Rollups are computed server-side and the first rows stream in before the query finishes.
  • Canvas waterfall, virtualized tables — 1.2M spans a day rendered without a janky scroll. The waterfall is one canvas, not twelve thousand DOM nodes.
  • Perf budget in CI — Lighthouse ≥ 95 or the build fails. We shipped at 98 and have stayed there.
03 — The hard part Rendering a lot of data fast, and staying readable when the reader is exhausted.

Legibility is the hard part, not volume

It is easy to ship a console that plots everything. The discipline was refusing to: every element on the overview had to earn its place by changing what an engineer would do next. That cut roughly two thirds of what the first prototype showed.

Cold cache, hot incident

The first meaningful paint of the screen is under 400ms and p95 query latency is 200ms — measured on a cold cache, because that is the state you are in during an incident, not the state you are in when you demo it. Achieved by streaming partial rollups rather than waiting for a complete answer.

Readability at 03:00

No colour-only encoding (one of Northbeam's on-call engineers is colour-blind), monospace numerals so columns align when scanning, and nothing that flashes. Contrast targets follow WCAG AA on the dark palette the tool ships with by default — because the people using it are in a dark room at an hour when a white background is physically unpleasant.

04 — Outcome Detected in half the time, at full data volume, under a budget that is enforced.

Mean time to detect fell 52% in the quarter Atlas shipped. The old dashboards were not deleted — they became drill-downs, reachable from the row they used to own.

The performance budget is the part that outlasted the project: it is a line in CI, so the number has not drifted. 1.2M spans a day at Lighthouse 98, and an on-call screen that answers the question before the engineer has finished asking it.
